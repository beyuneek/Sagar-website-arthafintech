You manage the properties. We manage the accounting behind them.

The problem

Property books rarely fail loudly. They drift.

Nothing breaks on a Tuesday. The close slips a few days, then a week. A reconciliation waits for next month. By the time it surfaces, it surfaces in a valuation, an audit, or a tax bill.

01

The close slips

Month-end lands weeks late. Owner reports go out late, and every decision that month is made on numbers that are already stale.

02

The classification is wrong

A roof replacement expensed instead of capitalized moves your NOI, your property valuation and your tax position — quietly, and for years.

03

The reconciliations pile up

Escrow, security deposits and inter-company balances sit unreconciled for months. Then the auditor finds them before you do.

What we handle

The detail behind the numbers.

Property accounting has its own vocabulary: escrow, CAM reconciliation, straight-line rent, capitalization thresholds, owner draws. We work in it every day.

Rent roll & lease tie-out

Rent roll agreed to the ledger every month, with abatements, escalations and straight-lining applied correctly.

Escrow, deposits & trust

Security deposits and escrow balances reconciled and segregated, so trust obligations hold up under audit.

CapEx vs repairs

A capitalization policy applied consistently. Maintain it, expense it. Improve it, capitalize it.

CAM & recoveries

Common area maintenance pools, tenant recovery calculations and year-end true-ups you can defend line by line.

Property closings

Settlement statements booked correctly at acquisition and disposal, with basis, prorations and loan costs in the right place.

Owner & investor reporting

Owner statements, distribution schedules and investor packs on a fixed date, in your format.

A month with us

What happens between the 1st and the 8th.

A standard monthly close calendar. We agree yours during transition, then work to it every month.

DAY 1–2

Cash first

  • Bank and credit card reconciliation
  • AP cut-off and accrual of unposted invoices
  • Cash receipts applied to tenant ledgers
DAY 3–4

Property tie-out

  • Rent roll agreed to the general ledger
  • Security deposit and escrow reconciliation
  • Delinquency and AR ageing review
DAY 5–6

Judgement calls

  • Accruals, prepaids and provisions
  • CapEx vs repairs review
  • Depreciation, loan interest and amortization
DAY 7–8

Out the door

  • Review and sign-off
  • Budget vs actual with commentary
  • Owner statements and reporting pack released
